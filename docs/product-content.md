# Sky Beats product and lesson content

## Product concept

Sky Beats is a co-learning companion used beside a Pioneer DJ DDJ-FLX4 connected to a Mac. It should feel more like a game than a course. Jess and Grace are both complete beginners. They change roles, help each other, finish short missions and collect shared visual rewards while learning enough to play a first set together.

Each guided lesson should take 5–10 minutes. Version 1 explains the controller action and lets the players confirm it themselves. A later version may use Web MIDI to detect supported controls.

There are no winners, individual scores, grades, accuracy percentages, speed rankings or penalties. The crew completes a mission when both players have tried their roles and choose to finish together.

## Main navigation

### Home

Purpose: show the duo, current mission and overall journey.

Content:

- Greeting: “Ready to make some noise?”
- Current mission card with lesson name, time and reward preview
- Primary action: “Take off”
- Flight-path progress through lessons 1–10
- Quick links to Practice and Rewards
- Continue state when a mission is unfinished

### Lesson

Purpose: teach one idea visually before practice.

Content pattern:

- One-sentence goal
- A required 20–30 second beginner briefing shown before any other teaching or challenge content:
  - **What it is:** define the idea in plain language.
  - **Why DJs use it:** explain the practical purpose without assuming DJ knowledge.
  - **What we’ll try together:** preview both players’ shared attempt.
- Optional “Show me” help that highlights or pictures the relevant DDJ-FLX4 control or rekordbox area
- Primary briefing action: “Got it — let’s try”
- A short “Listen / Look / Do” explanation
- Optional 10–20 second demonstration or count-in
- Primary action: “Try it”
- Secondary action: “Hear that again”

Every future mission must provide non-empty `what`, `why` and `together` briefing content. The briefing comes before Listen / Look / Do and before the controller challenge. It teaches both beginners; it never asks either person to explain the idea to the other.

### Challenge

Purpose: guide a short turn-based action on the controller.

Content pattern:

- Challenge title
- Active player avatar and “[Name] on the controls”
- A visible helper job for the other player, such as counting, listening or giving the ready signal
- One instruction at a time
- Clear completion control for manual confirmation
- Role-swap moment where relevant
- Primary completion action: “Land the mix”
- Optional “Help each other” hint and no-penalty retry

### Practice

Purpose: repeat a chosen skill without affecting lesson order.

Skill cards:

- Beat counting
- Cue points
- Phrase spotting
- EQ practice
- Transitions
- Loops
- Effects
- Track selection

### Rewards

Purpose: display earned patches, gear and shared milestones.

Sections:

- Patches
- Gear
- Gig posters

All rewards belong to the Sky Beats crew rather than to an individual player. Locked rewards show the shared mission that reveals them, without manipulative countdowns or artificial scarcity.

## Lesson path

### 1. Beat Basics — Find the Beat

- **Goal:** Tap along and hear the repeating 1-2-3-4 pulse.
- **What it is:** The beat is the steady pulse underneath the music—the part your feet naturally tap to.
- **Why DJs use it:** DJs follow the beat so two tracks can move together without sounding messy.
- **What we’ll try together:** Listen for the pulse, count 1–2–3–4, and make beat 1 the strongest count.
- **Jess role:** Start the track and count aloud while Grace listens for the pulse.
- **Grace role:** Tap the four beats while Jess quietly keeps the count going.
- **Duo challenge:** Swap roles and land together on beat 1 three times.
- **Success line:** “Beat found. Your feet knew all along.”
- **Reward:** Beat Finder patch.

### 2. Bars & Phrases — Drop on the 1

- **Goal:** Recognise a new four-beat bar and the start of a musical section.
- **What it is:** Beats arrive in small groups called bars, and bars join into bigger musical sections called phrases.
- **Why DJs use it:** Starting a new track at the beginning of a musical section makes a mix feel planned and natural.
- **What we’ll try together:** Count the beats, listen for a change, and give the ready signal just before the next beat 1.
- **Grace role:** Count four beats while Jess gets ready over Play.
- **Jess role:** Press Play on the next beat 1 while Grace gives the ready signal.
- **Duo challenge:** Call “ready” before the next phrase and start together.
- **Success line:** “Clean landing. Right on the 1.”
- **Reward:** Flight-path sticker.

### 3. Cue It — Ready, Set, Cue

- **Goal:** Use Cue and Play, then set a simple cue point.
- **What it is:** A cue point is a saved starting place inside a track.
- **Why DJs use it:** It lets a DJ prepare the exact moment they want before the audience hears the track.
- **What we’ll try together:** Find a clear first beat, save it with Cue, move away, and return to it.
- **Jess role:** Follow the prompt to try Cue and Play while Grace listens for what changes.
- **Grace role:** Set and return to the cue point while Jess gives the ready signal.
- **Duo challenge:** Prepare a track for the other player without starting it.
- **Success line:** “Track parked and ready.”
- **Reward:** Cue Master patch.

### 4. Match the Energy — Pick the Next Track

- **Goal:** Choose a next track with a similar or deliberate energy level.
- **What it is:** Energy describes how calm, busy, gentle or exciting a track feels.
- **Why DJs use it:** Track order helps shape how the music journey rises, rests and builds again.
- **What we’ll try together:** Listen to two options and agree whether the next track should lift, hold or settle the mood. There is no single correct answer.
- **Grace role:** Choose between two track cards while Jess listens to both.
- **Jess role:** Choose whether the energy rises, stays steady or settles; Grace adds what she hears.
- **Duo challenge:** Agree on a three-track mini journey.
- **Success line:** “Good call. The dance floor stays with you.”
- **Reward:** Headphone colour.

### 5. First Transition — Pass the Sound

- **Goal:** Blend two tracks using channel volume or the crossfader.
- **What it is:** A transition is the moment one track hands the music over to another.
- **Why DJs use it:** A smooth handover keeps the music flowing instead of stopping between songs.
- **What we’ll try together:** One person counts eight beats while the other slowly moves the sound from deck A to deck B, then swap roles.
- **Jess role:** Start deck A and cue deck B while Grace watches the controls and gives the ready signal.
- **Grace role:** Bring deck B in slowly while Jess counts eight steady beats.
- **Duo challenge:** Swap roles and repeat.
- **Success line:** “Two tracks, one smooth flight.”
- **Reward:** Transition Ace patch.

### 6. Bass Swap — Trade the Low End

- **Goal:** Avoid clashing bass by swapping low EQ between tracks.
- **Grace role:** Lower deck B’s low EQ while Jess points out the labelled control.
- **Jess role:** Swap the bass at the phrase change while Grace counts towards beat 1.
- **Duo challenge:** Repeat with roles reversed.
- **Success line:** “Bass swapped. No sonic stampede.”
- **Reward:** Bass Pilot patch.

### 7. Loop It — Catch and Release

- **Goal:** Set, hear and release a basic loop.
- **Jess role:** Choose a safe loop point with Grace listening for a clear beat.
- **Grace role:** Activate and release the loop while Jess counts towards beat 1.
- **Duo challenge:** Use one loop to create extra transition time.
- **Success line:** “Loop caught. Loop released.”
- **Reward:** Loop Legend patch.

### 8. Effects Lab — Tasteful Chaos

- **Goal:** Try one effect and learn that less is usually more.
- **Grace role:** Pick an effect and describe the sound she expects.
- **Jess role:** Control its amount while Grace gives the clean-off signal.
- **Duo challenge:** Make one silly version and one subtle version.
- **Success line:** “Science confirms: that was spicy.”
- **Reward:** Lightning controller sticker.

### 9. Build a Set — Plan the Flight

- **Goal:** Arrange 4–6 tracks into a short musical journey.
- **Jess choice:** Add three tracks.
- **Grace choice:** Add three tracks.
- **Duo challenge:** Name the set and assign each transition.
- **Success line:** “Flight plan locked.”
- **Reward:** Duo name badge.

### 10. First Gig — Play It Together

- **Goal:** Perform and optionally record a 10–15 minute set.
- **Duo structure:** Alternate transitions and help each other recover from mistakes.
- **Finish prompt:** “Ready to land the mix?”
- **Success line:** “First gig complete. You made that together.”
- **Reward:** First Gig patch and personalised gig poster.

## Set Builder content

The post-MVP Set Builder lets each player choose three tracks, order them and assign transitions. It creates a mission card such as:

> Start with Grace’s track. Jess performs transition 1. Grace performs transition 2. Use one loop somewhere ridiculous. Finish together.

Name prompts:

- Cloud Disco
- Snacktime Takeover
- Purple Headphones Club
- Turbulence on the Dance Floor
- Make your own

## Safety and privacy notes

- Encourage comfortable listening levels and breaks.
- Never require personal data, photos, voice recordings or public profiles for the MVP.
- Keep any recording feature local and opt-in, with an obvious delete action.
- Avoid unsafe instructions involving cables or controller setup while equipment is powered.
- A grown-up should manage software installation, audio routing and account-connected music services.
