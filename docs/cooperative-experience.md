# Cooperative experience rules

## The central rule

Jess and Grace are one Sky Beats crew. They never compete against each other, and the app never decides that one of them is the better DJ.

The app may change who is touching the controller, but it must never turn that into two separate tests. When one person is on the controls, the other has a visible, useful helper job.

## Beginner promise

Assume both players are complete beginners. Sky Beats—not Jess—provides the teaching. Neither person must demonstrate a skill before learning it, and neither person is responsible for correcting the other.

Each mission introduces one small skill, revisits something already learned and ends while the experience is still fun. Technical vocabulary should appear only when the app immediately points to the relevant control and lets the pair try it.

## Cooperative mission pattern

Every mission follows this rhythm:

1. **Brief together:** in about 20–30 seconds, explain “What it is,” “Why DJs use it” and “What we’ll try together” in plain language. Offer an optional control picture or highlight, then wait for “Got it — let’s try.”
2. **First role:** one player uses the controller; the partner counts, listens, points or gives the ready signal.
3. **Swap roles:** repeat the skill with the jobs reversed.
4. **Crew moment:** complete one tiny action together.
5. **Celebrate together:** unlock one shared crew reward.

Example:

- “Jess: slowly move the channel fader.”
- “Grace: count eight steady beats.”
- “Now swap jobs.”
- “Together: choose when to land the mix.”

## Completion rules

- Completion means both players had a go; it does not mean technical perfection.
- Use manual confirmation in the MVP. Trust the players when they tap Done.
- Never show accuracy scores, grades, star ratings, speed comparisons or individual totals.
- Never name a winner, fastest player, best mix or most valuable player.
- Retrying is always optional and has no cost.
- Let the pair pause or skip a difficult step and return later.
- Repeating a completed mission is practice, not remediation.
- A missed beat is described neutrally: “A little early” or “A little late,” followed by one helpful cue.
- If hardware detection is added later, use it to recognise actions and offer hints—not to judge performance.

## Rewards

Rewards are deliberately motivating, but always shared:

- Each completed mission adds a patch, sticker, outfit item or poster to the crew collection.
- Use “We earned” and “Collected together,” not “Jess won” or “Grace beat.”
- Preview the next reward to create anticipation.
- Keep rewards visual and immediate; avoid points or complicated economies.
- Do not remove rewards, break streaks or punish time away.
- Keep locked items friendly: “Complete this crew mission to reveal it.”
- Celebrate effort, role-swapping, listening and helping—not only technically clean mixes.

## Encouragement ladder

When the pair needs support, reveal help gradually:

1. Repeat the same short instruction.
2. Highlight or picture the exact DDJ-FLX4 control.
3. Add a count or physical cue.
4. Offer “Do it together.”
5. Offer “Park this one for later.”

Never use red failure screens, buzzers, sad avatars or copy such as “wrong,” “failed” or “try harder.”

## Language guide

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| “Your score” | “Crew progress” |
| “Jess’s result” | “What we tried” |
| “Winner” | “Mission complete” |
| “Perfect!” | “You found it together!” |
| “You failed” | “That one got wobbly. Want a hint or another go?” |
| “Grace’s test” | “Grace on the controls; Jess on the count” |
| “Beat your best” | “Try a smoother round together” |
| “3 attempts remaining” | “Take as many goes as you like” |

## Required interface states

- **Briefing:** one goal, reward preview and “Take off together.”
- **Controller role:** both names visible; one control instruction and one helper instruction.
- **Role swap:** affirm the first attempt, then visibly exchange jobs.
- **Duo action:** both avatars and one shared instruction.
- **Need help:** repeat, picture the control, do it together or park it.
- **Completion:** shared language, shared reward and a choice between stopping or another mission.
- **Return visit:** no guilt about elapsed time; simply offer to continue.

## Cooperative acceptance checks

A mission is ready only when:

- both players have an active role throughout;
- neither role assumes prior DJ knowledge;
- there is no individual scoring or comparison;
- hints are kind, specific and optional;
- retrying or leaving has no penalty;
- the reward belongs to the crew;
- the final message credits what they did together.
